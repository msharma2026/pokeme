After our final round of interviews, the biggest thing we took away was that getting people together is hard. Finding enough players at the right time and skill level is a genuine pain point, and users responded well to the idea of an app that could solve that.

What surprised us most was how our original main feature, the 1-on-1 poke matchmaking system, ended up being less popular than the meetup feature. Users pointed out that they were uncomfortable meeting a single stranger player and play with them. Group meetups ended up becoming the main feature of the app. They felt more relevant to our users and allowed more flexibility. The meetups are also quick and easy to plan, which was what our users liked the most about them. Users also it clear they didn't want to plan ahead. They wanted to open the app, see something happening, and join. 
The one thing holding the app back is user base size. A few people mentioned that the app is only as good as the number of people using it, and just like professor Aaron from the Student Startup Center told us in class, the hardest part about a product like ours is to get customers when we first launch it.
